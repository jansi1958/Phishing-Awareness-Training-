1. What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where a criminal pretends to be someone you trust — your bank, your college, a delivery company, even your boss — to trick you into handing over passwords, OTPs, card details or money, or into installing malware.

It works not by breaking technology, but by exploiting human urgency: "Your account will be closed in 2 hours!", "You have won a prize!", "Verify now or lose access!"

Key fact: Most phishing messages are not addressed to you personally. They are sent to millions of people — the attacker only needs a few to take the bait.

2. Types of phishing

📧 Email phishing

Mass emails impersonating banks, popular services or employers, with a malicious link or attachment.

🎯 Spear phishing

Targeted messages that use your name, role or recent activity to look convincing. Researched just for you.

📱 Smishing

Phishing by SMS or WhatsApp: fake delivery updates, KYC warnings, refund links and "part-time job" offers.

📞 Vishing

Voice calls from fake "bank officers" or "support agents" asking for OTPs, PINs or remote-access apps.

🪞 Clone / fake websites

Pixel-perfect copies of real login pages. The only giveaway is usually the address bar.

📎 Attachment phishing

Invoices, "resumes" or PDFs that install malware or open a fake login page when opened.

3. Red flags — how to spot it

  • Urgency or threats — "act now", "account blocked", "legal action". Real organisations rarely pressure you like this.
  • Sender address doesn't match — the name says your bank, the actual address is a random Gmail or a look-alike domain (hdfc-secure-login.xyz).
  • Links that don't match — hover (or long-press) before you tap. The text says one site, the link goes somewhere else.
  • Requests for secrets — no bank, college or company will ever ask for your password, PIN, CVV or full OTP.
  • Too-good offers — prizes, refunds or jobs you never applied for.
  • Generic greeting — "Dear Customer" instead of your name, plus spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Unexpected attachments — especially .zip, .exe, .apk or "invoice" files you weren't expecting.

Example — spot the problems

From: SBI Bank <sbi.kyc-update@gmail-secure.xyz>
Subject: URGENT!! Your account will be BLOCKED today

Dear Customer, your KYC is expired. Verify your PAN and OTP immediately at the link below or your account will be closed permanently.
http://sbi-kyc-verify.free-site.in/login

Problems: free email domain pretending to be a bank, threat + urgency, generic greeting, asks for an OTP, and the link is not the bank's real domain.

4. Do's and Don'ts

✅ Do

  • Check the sender's full email address, not just the display name.
  • Hover / long-press links to preview the real destination.
  • Go to the official app or type the website address yourself.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) everywhere you can.
  • Verify unusual requests through a second channel (call the person).
  • Report suspicious messages instead of just deleting them.

🚫 Don't

  • Never share OTPs, PINs, CVVs or passwords — with anyone.
  • Don't click links in unexpected messages about money, KYC or prizes.
  • Don't install apps (APK files, screen-sharing apps) a caller asks for.
  • Don't use the same password on multiple sites.
  • Don't trust a page just because it has a padlock — phishing sites have HTTPS too.

6. Already clicked? Act fast

  1. Disconnect — if you downloaded something, disconnect from the internet.
  2. Change the password — from a different, clean device. Then change it anywhere you reused it.
  3. Turn on / reset 2FA and sign out all other sessions.
  4. Call your bank if you entered card or banking details — block cards if needed.
  5. Report it — in India, report cyber fraud on cybercrime.gov.in or call the helpline 1930 immediately (speed matters for recovering money).
  6. Tell your IT team / college if it was a work or college account.
